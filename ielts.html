When Maps were Made for the Public

Since the art of map-making began, maps have largely been made for explorers, academics, and rulers. It wasn’t until the 19th century that the general public began to demand maps for themselves. More than anything else, it was the appeal of travel to ordinary people that encouraged publishing companies to begin creating and printing more maps than ever before, in order to meet the demand of their new market.

In the years after the American Civil War (1861-65), the rapidly growing US railroad system had so many independent rail companies, schedules, and destinations that maps were critical for planning a person’s journey. One publisher, Rand McNally, made a fortune from producing railway maps for different parts of the USA, combined with a timetable and many descriptions of scenery and towns in the same booklet.

By the 1880s, maps for the general public were being produced in even greater numbers. A London-based publisher, George Philip, was one of the first to produce a map suitable for the new pastime of cycling. This was a map that could not be damaged by water. Another cycling map, designed by George Blum, was also becoming very popular. Blum’s map was special because it showed the kind of surface the paths had, and whether they were flat or hilly.

By 1900, the growing popularity of the automobile was creating new demands for road maps. André Michelin, one of the brothers who owned the Michelin tyre company in France, saw this as an opportunity to promote both motoring and his business. The company began to produce a guide for motorists which included a map, information about finding economical accommodation, what to do if your car broke down, and how to change a tyre. The Michelin guide became particularly well-known for helping people select a good restaurant.

In 1917, Rand McNally produced a new kind of map for motorists, called ‘Auto Trails Maps’. These were different because they helped people explore roads through a particular region of the USA, not just major cities. Around this time, the company was also responsible for giving numbers to American roads, which made them easier to find on a map.

By the 1920s, oil companies saw an opportunity to give away free maps as a way of encouraging people to drive more and therefore buy more fuel. However, this stopped in the 1970s because the high price of oil meant that it was no longer economical to give maps away for free.

In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, map-making was still a complex and expensive process, but it was being transformed by new technology. The introduction of a process called lithography meant that maps could be produced more cheaply and in larger numbers than ever before. It also meant that maps could include more detail and be more attractive to look at.

The growth in world tourism from the 1920s led to another new type of map, one designed especially for tourists. The ‘Touraide’, first produced in 1926, was a map that was organised according to the particular needs of each traveller, with special routes and information included. Maps designed for pilots also became available during this period, although there is no information about whether they helped improve safety records for flights.

In the 1930s, map-making for the general public took another leap forward when Harry Beck produced his famous map of the London Underground. Early maps of the Underground had tried to show the actual geographical layout of the railway lines, which made them very difficult to read. Beck’s map was not geographically accurate, but showed the lines and stations in a clear way, using straight lines and bright colours. At first, his map was not well-received, but it later became the model for public transport maps around the world.

Another important map-maker was Phyllis Pearsall, who spent a year walking 23,000 streets to create a street map of London. In 1936, she published the first A-Z atlas of London, which was small, clear, and easy to use. There is no information about whether she was the first woman to produce a published map, but her atlas was a great success and is still popular today.
